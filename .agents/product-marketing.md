## Stable Product Truth

### Current product overview

`FACT`

Archestimator is a pre-release AEC application that connects structured estimate creation with proposal publication, browser review, supported discussion, and an explicit recipient response.

### Current category candidates

- `FOUNDER OBSERVATION`: Construction cost intelligence software.
- `EVIDENCE-BACKED INFERENCE`: Estimating and proposal workflow.
- `WORKING HYPOTHESIS`: Commercial proposal and decision layer for AEC project pricing. This is internal strategy language, not public feature scope.

### Current users and roles

- `FACT`: Signed-in proposers create and manage estimates and published proposal versions.
- `FACT`: Proposal recipients review shared proposal context and, when authorized through a personalized link, can discuss and submit a supported response.
- `RESEARCH GAP`: The repository does not establish a validated profession-specific beachhead or active customer mix.

### Current launch status

`FACT`

Access is invite or allowlist gated. Current runtime behavior supports `closed beta`; the shared control header's mobile menu still says `Private alpha`, so public release wording is not fully reconciled. The `/new` body avoids a phase label and says access is limited and available by request.

### Verified proof points

`FACT`

Current source and tests verify structured estimate models, reusable components and templates, editable XLSX output, proposal publication, browser review, optional password protection, supported contextual comments, and the three explicit response states. These are implementation proof points, not proof of hosted reliability or customer outcomes.

### What exists

- Structured estimates with named project groups, areas, editable priced rows, quantities, and cost context.
- Reusable cost components, variants, supplier or source context, and estimate templates.
- Editable XLSX proposal export.
- Proposal publication that selects estimate content and creates a version intended for review.
- Browser-based shared proposal review with optional password protection.
- Overview, Estimate, Financials, Discussion, and Decision review views.
- Comments within supported proposal and estimate contexts.
- Current response states labeled `Approve proposal`, `Request changes`, and `Decline`, subject to recipient role authorization.
- Decisions associated with the reviewed version or content identity.
- Invite or allowlist access controls consistent with a closed beta.

- Purchase orders.
- Invoicing.

- Legally binding records.

## Working positioning experiment for `/new`

### Working hypothesis

`WORKING HYPOTHESIS`

Archestimator connects project-specific pricing work with the proposal review and decision that follows it.

### External category

`EVIDENCE-BACKED INFERENCE`

Estimating and proposal workflow for businesses in the AEC industry.

### Internal strategic thesis

`WORKING HYPOTHESIS`

Archestimator is the commercial proposal and decision layer for AEC project pricing.

This is internal strategy language. It must not imply transactions, accounting, contracts, purchase orders, procurement, invoicing, or legal issuance.

### Working positioning statement

`WORKING HYPOTHESIS`

For businesses in the AEC industry that repeatedly prepare and send project-specific pricing, Archestimator is an estimating and proposal workflow that connects structured estimate creation, controlled proposal review, discussion, and client decisions. Unlike spreadsheets, exported files, email, and messaging applications used separately, Archestimator keeps the pricing work and the commercial response in one product context.

### Approved one-liner

`FOUNDER OBSERVATION`

Archestimator helps businesses in the AEC industry to build structured cost estimates and turn them into client-ready proposals that can be reviewed, discussed, and decided in one connected workflow.

## Commercial Roles

- **Proposer:** The AEC business preparing and publishing project-specific pricing. This is the likely paying product user.
- **Reviewer:** The recipient evaluating the proposal, such as a client, buyer, owner, contractor, procurement reviewer, or another AEC business.
- **Relationship:** Two roles in one proposal workflow, not two separate product markets.
- **Boundary:** Reviewer language must not default to homeowner or architect-to-client work.

## Jobs to Be Done

### Main job

`WORKING HYPOTHESIS`

When another party asks my business to price a project scope, package, product, or service, I want to turn the scope and our cost knowledge into a clear version for review, resolve questions against that version, and receive an explicit response, so I can move the opportunity forward without losing control of the price, revision, or decision.

### Job dimensions

- **Functional:** Structure and price an estimate, publish a review version, discuss it, and capture a response.
- **Emotional:** Feel confident that the recipient sees the intended version and that questions are not separated from the pricing context.
- **Social:** Appear commercially prepared and accountable to a client, buyer, owner, or project partner.

### Switching forces

- **Push:** Rework, version ambiguity, scattered questions, and a response separated from the reviewed price.
- **Pull:** A connected working context from estimate through explicit response.
- **Habit:** Spreadsheet templates, exported files, email, messaging, phone calls, and manual follow-up.
- **Anxiety:** Recipient adoption, estimating flexibility, pricing exposure, workflow migration, and whether the shared review link fits real commercial behavior.

## Competitive Alternative

`WORKING HYPOTHESIS`

A spreadsheet for pricing, a file for delivery, and email or chat for review and decision.

Alternative clusters:

1. Pricing in Excel, Google Sheets, estimating software, or accounting quotation modules.
2. Delivery through PDF, workbook, document, or email attachment.
3. Discussion and response through email, chat, phone calls, or verbal approval.
4. Status quo through file naming, memory, manual follow-up, or doing nothing.

The experiment does not attack Excel. Archestimator supports editable XLSX output and positions its added value around structure, publication, discussion, and decision context.

## Differentiation And Value

### Primary differentiation

`EVIDENCE-BACKED INFERENCE`

Archestimator carries structured estimate information into a published proposal review surface where discussion and explicit response can remain attached to the proposal context.

This is a product-mechanics distinction, not a verified claim of competitive uniqueness.

### Value hierarchy

1. **Primary value:** One connected commercial workflow from working price to reviewed response.
2. **Supporting value:** Structured and reusable estimating work.
3. **Supporting value:** Controlled proposal review, contextual discussion, and explicit response.

## Objection Architecture

### Does Archestimator replace Excel?

Current answer: No. Editable XLSX output remains available. Archestimator adds project structure, reusable cost knowledge, proposal publication, discussion, and decision context around the working document.

### Can it fit how our business structures estimates or quotations?

Current answer: Estimates support named groups and areas, flexible cost rows, reusable components, and templates. The page does not claim one universal profession-specific hierarchy.

### Does the proposal recipient need to install anything?

Current answer: No software install. Review happens in the browser. Discussion and response actions use the recipient's personalized secure link.

### Is Archestimator available now?

Current answer: Access is limited and available by request while Archestimator prepares the workflow for broader testing with AEC businesses. The acquisition action is `Request early access`.

## Research Gaps

1. Tell me about the last project quote, estimate, or proposal you sent to an outside party.
2. How did you build the pricing and decide it was ready to send?
3. What did you send, to whom, and how did they ask questions or request changes?
4. How did you know which version they were reviewing, and what happened after the price changed?
5. How did you learn that it was approved, declined, or still waiting, and what did you do next?

These questions ask about past behavior. They do not ask whether someone likes the positioning or says they would use the product.
