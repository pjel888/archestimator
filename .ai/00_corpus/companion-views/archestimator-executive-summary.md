# Archestimator Executive Summary

Archestimator is framed as a pre-launch MVP for the AEC market in the Philippines and Southeast Asia, positioned as a construction cost estimating platform and, at the brand level, as a "cost intelligence layer." [file:1] The document presents itself as the single working startup corpus covering brand, product, pricing, business model, marketing, technical architecture, launch readiness, and operations, while also clarifying that Notion remains the canonical home for finalized decisions and live trackers. [file:1]

## Business and positioning

The file’s central strategic claim is that cost estimates contain reusable commercial knowledge such as supplier choices, pricing logic, revisions, and approval history, but that this intelligence usually dies inside spreadsheets. [file:1] Archestimator is positioned as the system that turns that dead-file workflow into reusable commercial infrastructure, with its strongest honest differentiation resting not on inventing reusable estimating intelligence, but on bringing it to spreadsheet-bound SMB AEC teams in PH and SEA, extending it from estimate creation to proposal approval, and eventually connecting it to supplier workflows. [file:1]

The target market is explicitly the AEC ecosystem, including architects, engineers, contractors, estimators, quantity surveyors, specialty and fit-out firms, interior designers, suppliers, project managers, developers, owners, procurement, and clients who review and approve cost proposals. [file:1] The wedge is the proposal-building side of the market, especially the people who build estimates daily and currently rely on spreadsheets. [file:1]

## Product definition

The live product is described as a structured estimate workspace built around a hierarchical estimate tree, reusable component library, templates, inline sheet editing, D3 project charts, shared proposal links, collaboration roles, activity logs, row version history, and Excel proposal export. [file:1] AI is no longer part of the launch product or current tiering, and the file is explicit that any future AI capability should remain deferred, labeled carefully, and never marketed as a present capability. [file:1]

The product story is organized around a broader roadmap chain: Estimate Workspace to Component Library to Supplier Catalog to Quote Requests to Product Comparison to Procurement or Commerce. [file:1] That roadmap is the bridge between the current MVP and the larger infrastructure thesis. [file:1]

## Pricing and business model

The business model starts with proposer-side SaaS subscriptions, with later expansion into client-side proposal-record subscriptions, enterprise portfolio plans, supplier catalog monetization, and eventually transaction-linked revenue. [file:1] Pricing is still exploratory and explicitly marked as a hypothesis, with a freemium, seat-based, PHP-denominated structure and a Solo plan anchored at ₱239 per month to align with local construction pricing psychology. [file:1]

The file repeatedly warns against pricing or claim overreach before validation. [file:1] It recommends validating price and tier design through discovery calls, beta usage, support observation, and a Van Westendorp price sensitivity exercise before publishing any public pricing page. [file:1]

## Brand and messaging

Brand language is disciplined around one unfamiliar concept, "cost intelligence layer," surrounded by plain, practical language aimed at tired estimators and proposal builders rather than abstract software buyers. [file:1] The tone system is called "Controlled Confidence," meaning Archestimator should sound precise, commercially credible, and quietly sharp, while avoiding hype, superiority theater, false uniqueness, or attacks on the user. [file:1]

The master brand promise is “You never start from zero again. Your numbers hold up,” and nearly every benefit is meant to ladder into two outcomes: time and intelligence. [file:1] In practice, the product promises faster estimate creation through reuse and stronger defensibility through activity and version history. [file:1]

## Technical and operational state

Technically, the product is described as a Next.js 16 and React 19 application using Supabase, PostgreSQL 17, strict TypeScript, Tailwind v4, TanStack Query, Zustand, Realtime collaboration, D3, and React Complex Tree. [file:1] The file also lays out clear trust boundaries: no BIM or drawing ingestion today, no autonomous estimating, no immutable issued-record claims, and no procurement, payment, or ERP execution yet. [file:1]

Operationally, the document expands beyond product and branding into launch readiness, legal setup, onboarding, analytics, support, billing, and finance. [file:1] Several of those areas remain unresolved and are explicitly flagged as needing founder decisions or real-world implementation, especially the company entity, legal documents, Data Privacy Act compliance, onboarding flow, analytics instrumentation, billing provider, and financial model. [file:1]

## Strategic reading

The document is best understood as a decision-and-alignment artifact rather than a final public narrative. [file:1] Its strongest function is to unify product truth, honest positioning, pricing logic, market sequence, and launch readiness into one working corpus, while forcing clear separation between what is already canon, what is proposed, what is only hypothesized, and what still requires input. [file:1]
