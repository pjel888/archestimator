# Information Architecture And Copy Plan

## Page Thesis

Archestimator is for teams whose quotes become proposal decisions.

The page should make the visitor understand that the product is not a blank spreadsheet, a generic quoting tool, or a broad ERP. It is a structured workspace where itemized quote logic, reusable cost intelligence, revision context, and proposal handoff stay connected.

### 4. Problem Narrative

Purpose:
- Name the pain without shaming spreadsheet users.
- Show why the product exists.

Headline:

> The hard part is not the total. It is keeping the logic behind the total intact.

Body:

> Quote work gets risky when supplier notes, price assumptions, scope changes, and proposal language live in different files. The waste feels normal: rechecking old rows, rebuilding repeated costs, comparing revisions by hand, and rewriting proposal context after the estimate is already done.

Proof bullets:

- Assumptions get separated from the number.
- Reusable cost logic gets rebuilt manually.
- Revisions become detective work.
- Proposal documents drift away from the estimate.
- Buyers review totals without the context needed to decide.

### 6. The Archestimator Method

Purpose:
- Define "the Archestimator way."
- Replace vague feature lists with a repeatable method.

Headline:

> Build the offer around the logic that will be reviewed.

Method steps:

1. `Structure the offer`
   Every quote starts from the project, package, product, service, supplier, scope, or work area.

2. `Price in context`
   Costs belong to a scope, source, quantity, rate, and assumption.

3. `Reuse what repeats`
   Products, assemblies, services, labor patterns, and scope packages should not be rebuilt every time.

4. `Keep revisions visible`
   Changes should be easier to review before they become buyer confusion.

5. `Hand off the proposal`
   The client-facing package should stay connected to the commercial logic that created it.

### 10. FAQ

Purpose:
- Answer objections before the final CTA.
- Use beta-specific language.

Rename heading:

> Before you request access

Questions:

1. `How does the closed beta work?`
   `Request access with the Google account you want enabled. Approved testers are allowlisted and onboarded into the workspace.`

2. `Who is the beta for?`
   `Teams that create itemized quotes, estimates, or proposal packages, especially construction, fit-out, supplier, and quote-heavy sales workflows.`

3. `Can I use this instead of Excel today?`
   `Use Archestimator alongside your current tools during beta. Spreadsheet/document handoff is part of the product direction and must be reviewed before commercial use.`

4. `Is this an AI estimator?`
   `No. Archestimator is estimator-led. AI, when enabled, assists review and explanation; it does not replace commercial judgment.`

5. `Is pricing final?`
   `No. Closed beta has no public payment flow. Pricing will be validated after beta discovery.`

## Copy Principles

- Be concrete before being clever.
- Explain commercial consequences, not only UI features.
- Use "proposal" for the client-facing output, "quote" for itemized offers, and "estimate" for the current proof workflow.
- Use "cost intelligence" as the strategic category language, but support it immediately with examples.
- Use "planned" early and often where public claims could overstate maturity.
- Do not say "all-in-one," "revolutionary," "seamless," "trusted by," or "AI-powered" as a primary claim.
