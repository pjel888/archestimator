## Decision question

Five blocked decisions on the Archestimator commercial-truth audit:

1. **F-11** — adjustment percentage range: UI `[-100, 100]` vs canonical `[-100, 1000]`. Which is truth?
2. **F-12** — XLSX Grand Total (with contingency+VAT) vs share page Proposal total (direct only) — ~23% divergence. Option (a) note, or option (b) extend snapshot?
3. **F-10** — currency validation breadth: 9-code set vs `^[A-Z]{3}$`. Expand or tighten?
4. **Wave 1** prioritization across 11 units.
5. **UNIT-001 test authorization** — grant TEST_TASK to fix the broken oracle fixture, or hold?

## Raw positions

**Architect (lead, in-context voice):**
- Wave 1: UNIT-001, UNIT-002, UNIT-003.
- F-11: widen UI to [-100, 1000] with visual warning above 100%.
- F-12: option (a) share-page note.
- F-10: tighten readers.
- UNIT-001: grant TEST_TASK.

**Skeptic:** Invert the burden of proof on F-11 and F-12; over-weighting test hygiene.
- F-11: tighten canonical DOWN to [-100, 100]. "Canonical" is an auditor's editorial label; the asymmetric range is what you get when someone wrote `min(-100)` and forgot `max`. The UI has shipped at [-100, 100] since day one; it is the de facto truth.
- F-12: option (a) is "shipping confusion with an apology stapled to it". Make the share page render the same total as XLSX; no note needed.
- UNIT-001: before granting TEST_TASK, ask whether labor removal was actually correct — the failing test could be a canary, not fixture rot.

**Pragmatist:** Ship the trust-killers, defer the refactor.
- Wave 1: UNIT-001 (grant TEST_TASK now, unconditional) + F-12 option (a) + F-10 tighten + F-11 narrow canonical.
- The broken oracle is the single highest-leverage 30 minutes on the board; CI is currently lying (2/3 red or skipped), so every other "canonical math is correct" claim is unverified.
- F-11 widen-UI is wrong: a 1000% adjustment on a construction line is a fat-finger that becomes a legally-binding XLSX you cannot unsend.
- **Surprise:** the "LOW / centavo drift" rating is mis-calibrated for PHP. PHP carries centavo-denominated retail pricing (₱100.25 × qty 3), so first users WILL hit the drift. Treat as HIGH for the target market.

**Critic:** F-12 (a) is worse than nothing.
- F-12 (a) converts negligence into willfulness — pre-beta is the cheap window for option (b) snapshot extension. Doing (a) now means doing (b) later, plus a migration, plus an apology.
- F-11: hard-block line adjustments above a sane threshold (e.g. 200%) with explicit override + reason + author; the 1000 canonical ceiling is likely arbitrary, not load-bearing.
- UNIT-001: decide labor-in-or-out BEFORE the TEST_TASK. "Fix the failing test" bakes in whoever's assumption wins the PR.
- **Surprise:** the 9-code currency set is missing CNY, JPY, KRW — all routine in PH construction material and equipment sourcing. "Tighten readers" rejects a legitimate JPY rebar quote at import time.

## Verdict

### Consensus points (where ≥3 of 4 voices aligned)

- **F-11 should NARROW the canonical to [-100, 100], not widen UI.** Skeptic+Pragmatist+Critic all converged here. Architect's initial position was wrong.
- **F-12 option (a) is a bandage, not a fix.** Skeptic+Critic+Pragmatist (partially) all rejected the note approach.
- **UNIT-001's TEST_TASK must be preceded by the labor-in-or-out decision.** Skeptic+Critic both flagged this as a potential canary.

### Strongest dissent

- The Critic's call to expand the currency set (CNY/JPY/KRW). The Skeptic and Pragmatist both said tighten. Real signal: PH construction sourcing does involve JPY/CNY rebar and equipment imports.

### Premise check (did the Skeptic challenge the question itself?)

- Yes, on F-11: "Canonical is an auditor's editorial label, not evidence." Forced verification against D1 schema.
- Yes, on F-01: "Was labor removal actually correct?" Forced verification against git history.

## Evidence-gathered after the council (premise-check responses)

### F-11 premise check — REFUTES Skeptic's "UI is de facto truth" but SUPPORTS narrow-canonical conclusion

- `migrations/0082_estimate_row_integrity_constraints.sql` DDL: `adjustment_percentage REAL NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (adjustment_percentage >= -100 AND adjustment_percentage <= 100)`.
- `tests/vitest/estimate-sheet-version-control-schema.test.ts:208` asserts: `check (adjustment_percentage >= -100 and adjustment_percentage <= 100)`.
- The D1 **storage layer** already enforces `[-100, 100]`.
- The `1000` maximum in `estimate-row-total.ts:14` and `actions.ts:884` (`maximum = mode === "discount" ? 100 : 1_000`) applies to **sheet-level contingency mode**, NOT per-row `adjustment_percentage`.
- **Conclusion:** F-11's original framing conflated two scales. The per-row `adjustment_percentage` should be `[-100, 100]` everywhere; the 1000 ceiling correctly lives on for sheet-level contingency only. Narrow the canonical per-row range to `[-100, 100]`.

### F-01 premise check — REFUTES Skeptic/Critic's "labor could be a canary"

- `git log` shows: `d9cf5b18e fix(estimates): remove dead labor cost feature from row totals and projections`.
- Explicit `fix(...)` commit message: deliberate removal as dead code.
- `grep -rn "labor" src/` returns zero matches.
- No D1 migration contains a labor column.
- **Conclusion:** labor removal was deliberate product-truth, not drift. The fixture is rot, not a canary. UNIT-001's TEST_TASK is safe to grant unconditionally.
