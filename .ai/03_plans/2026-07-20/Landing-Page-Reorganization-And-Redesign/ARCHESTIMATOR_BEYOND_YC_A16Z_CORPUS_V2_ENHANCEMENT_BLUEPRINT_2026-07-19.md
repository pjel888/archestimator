## Corpus V2 Enhancement Blueprint

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 19 July 2026  
**Purpose:** Identify the next strategic improvements to the Archestimator Brand, Business & Startup Corpus without weakening the product truth, broad AEC ambition, or approved brand direction.

# Executive Verdict

The current corpus is already unusually strong for a pre-release startup. It defines the category, product descriptor, market, benefits, product truth, founder advantage, GTM, pricing hypotheses, metrics, roadmap, claims discipline, and pitch narrative in one governed document.

The next improvement is **not more generic startup advice and not more prose**.

The next improvement is to turn the corpus from a comprehensive description of the company into an **executable company-design system** that answers five operating questions:

1. **When should the market think of Archestimator?**
2. **Why should someone switch from their current workflow?**
3. **What exact product experience proves the value?**
4. **How does one successful workflow create the next user, project, or account?**
5. **How do positioning, product, sales, pricing, and evidence update together?**

The highest-leverage strategic idea is:

> **Widen the attack vector through buying situations, not by making the public message broader.**

Archestimator can retain its full target market—estimators, architects, engineers, project and construction managers, contractors, design-build teams, commercial interiors, proposal owners, suppliers, clients, developers, and procurement—while becoming associated with a set of concrete situations shared across that market.

The market is broad. The product entry is specific. The brand can be mentally available in many relevant moments without becoming vague.

## Ten additions that would materially improve the corpus

1. A formal **positioning canvas** built from real competitive alternatives.
2. A **Category Entry Point map** defining the buying situations Archestimator should own.
3. A **switching-forces model** covering both desire and anxiety around leaving Excel-led workflows.
4. A multi-stage **activation and First Impact ladder**.
5. A causal **growth-loop architecture**, including recipient-led distribution.
6. A precise **user, champion, buyer, approver, recipient, and blocker map**.
7. A **revenue architecture and discovery system** built around customer impact and critical events.
8. A **pricing research system** rather than permanent packaging assumptions.
9. A **distinctive brand-asset system** beyond logo and color.
10. A **proof library and corpus governance layer** that turns every claim into an evidence-backed operating input.

# Part I — Research Lenses Beyond YC and a16z

## 1. Deliberate positioning: April Dunford

April Dunford’s positioning method separates positioning into five dependent components:

1. Competitive alternatives;
2. differentiated capabilities;
3. customer value and proof;
4. target-customer characteristics;
5. market category.

The key lesson for Archestimator is that positioning should begin with what customers would do without the product—not with a large list of roles or a feature inventory.

For Archestimator, the most important alternative is usually not another startup. It is:

> **Excel or another spreadsheet + copied templates + exported documents + email or messaging + manual version control.**

That is the baseline against which buyers judge whether Archestimator is meaningfully better.

## 2. Demand-side understanding: Jobs to Be Done

Jobs-to-be-Done research focuses on why people switch—not merely what features they request. The relevant forces are:

- the push away from the current situation;
- the pull toward the new solution;
- the habit of the current method;
- the anxiety of the new method.

Archestimator’s corpus documents the push and pull well. It does not yet sufficiently document the forces that keep users in Excel and email:

- existing estimate libraries;
- familiar formulas and shortcuts;
- client expectations;
- fear of losing control of the number;
- uncertainty about migration;
- concern that collaborators or clients will refuse a new tool;
- fear of weak internet or unavailable files;
- concern that software will impose a workflow that does not match the project.

These are not secondary objections. They determine adoption.

## 3. Broad-market brand growth: Category Entry Points

Research from the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute and LinkedIn’s B2B Institute provides a useful resolution to the apparent tension between a broad market and focused messaging.

A brand can target a broad category while building associations with multiple **Category Entry Points**: recurring situations, needs, motives, or problems that cause a buyer to enter the category.

This is directly applicable to Archestimator.

Instead of treating thirteen roles as thirteen unrelated markets, Archestimator can become easy to remember in recurring situations that cut across those roles:

- a new estimate must be created;
- a prior estimate needs to be reused;
- a bid or proposal deadline is approaching;
- a client needs a clearer cost breakdown;
- scope changed and the number must be revised;
- someone is unsure which version is current;
- a cost increase must be explained;
- multiple people need to review the proposal;
- a client response or decision needs to be recorded;
- a firm wants consistent estimating across projects.

This creates a wider attack vector without making the hero a list of professions or features.

## 4. Mental availability and distinctive assets

The corpus defines a strong verbal identity, but it does not yet define a disciplined system of distinctive assets.

Research on distinctive brand assets emphasizes long-term consistency, uniqueness, and recognizability. A startup does not need dozens of brand devices. It needs a small number that are repeatedly and coherently used.

For Archestimator, candidate assets include:

- the established orange;
- the Archestimator mark and wordmark;
- a ledger or estimate-grid motif;
- an orange “connected cost thread” linking estimate, proposal, comment, revision, and decision;
- a specific treatment of project totals and cost rows;
- a consistent editorial typography system;
- a repeated verbal structure around keeping the estimate connected.

These need design rules, not constant reinvention.

## 5. Product-market fit and activation

The Superhuman product-market-fit method measures whether users who have experienced the core product would be very disappointed if it disappeared. The important detail is that the survey should target users who have actually experienced the core value, not visitors or lightly engaged signups.

Archestimator therefore needs a precise definition of “experienced the core value.” A first valid total is necessary, but it does not prove the differentiated product promise.

A stronger progression is:

1. First valid estimate;
2. first client-facing export or published proposal;
3. first recipient view or response;
4. first revision completed in context;
5. first repeated estimate or reuse event.

The PMF survey should only become a serious signal after a user has crossed at least the client-handoff stage.

## 6. Product-led growth and time to value

Product-led frameworks emphasize a defined activation flow, a clear “aha” moment, and short time to value. Archestimator should not blindly copy consumer-style self-service because construction cost work is complex and trust-sensitive. However, the principle still applies:

> **Every first-run step should move the user toward a credible result, not teach the whole interface.**

For Archestimator, this supports:

- a guided sample as the fastest demonstration of value;
- a concierge import or spreadsheet migration path;
- a real sample proposal;
- progressive disclosure of advanced features;
- an activation path organized around the first estimate and client handoff.

## 7. Growth loops and defensibility

Reforge distinguishes compounding growth loops from linear funnel thinking. NFX distinguishes network effects from other forms of defensibility such as embedding, brand, scale, and data.

This is important for claim discipline.

Archestimator may have:

- workflow embedding;
- switching costs from accumulated user-owned data;
- recipient-led distribution;
- team expansion;
- data compounding inside a workspace;
- future multi-party network potential.

It does **not yet have a proven network effect** merely because proposals are shared with other people. A sharing loop is distribution. A network effect exists only when the product itself becomes more valuable as network participation increases.

The corpus should explicitly distinguish:

- distribution loop;
- retention loop;
- data flywheel;
- collaboration value;
- network effect.

## 8. Revenue architecture and customer impact

Winning by Design’s SPICED framework organizes customer understanding around:

- Situation;
- Pain;
- Impact;
- Critical Event;
- Decision.

This is a better structure for Archestimator’s founder-led sales than a general list of discovery questions.

A construction-cost buying decision is often triggered by a critical event:

- bid deadline;
- client presentation;
- tender submission;
- budget approval;
- scope change;
- change-order negotiation;
- procurement review;
- need to standardize an internal workflow.

The sales system should diagnose those events and the business consequences rather than immediately demonstrating features.

Winning by Design also defines “First Impact” as the true end of onboarding. For Archestimator, First Impact should be tied to a real construction-cost outcome, not account setup.

## 9. Pricing as product strategy

Simon-Kucher’s monetization work emphasizes willingness-to-pay conversations early enough to influence product and packaging—not after the product is considered finished.

The corpus correctly treats pricing as unvalidated. The next improvement is to make pricing research more operational.

The question is not simply, “How much would you pay?” It is:

- which outcome creates value;
- who experiences that value;
- who controls the budget;
- which capability changes willingness to pay;
- which metric scales with customer value;
- which limits would suppress adoption;
- what is easy to understand and administer.

# Part III — The Missing Strategic Systems

## 11. A formal positioning canvas

The corpus contains the ingredients of positioning but does not yet display their causal relationship.

### Competitive alternatives

What the customer would use if Archestimator did not exist:

1. Excel or Google Sheets;
2. copied internal estimate templates;
3. Word, PDF, or presentation software for the client proposal;
4. email, messaging, or calls for review and revision;
5. manual folders and filenames for version history;
6. general project-management software;
7. specialist takeoff or estimating suites;
8. enterprise construction platforms;
9. custom internal systems;
10. doing nothing and accepting the fragmentation.

### Differentiated capabilities

Capabilities Archestimator can credibly emphasize relative to the common status quo:

- estimate creation continues into client-facing review;
- comments and decisions remain attached to the proposal context;
- revisions remain connected to the estimate;
- user-owned cost components and structures can be reused;
- Excel remains a first-class output;
- the workflow is AEC-specific;
- the product can serve authors, project coordinators, and recipients without pretending they perform the same job;
- future intelligence is built on private structured project context rather than pooled customer estimates.

### Value themes

- less reconstruction and reformatting;
- clearer cost communication;
- fewer disconnected conversations;
- more controlled revisions;
- faster movement toward a client or project decision;
- accumulating value from prior work.

### Best-fit customer characteristics

This is not a narrow market definition. It is a description of the conditions under which Archestimator is most valuable:

- repeatedly creates itemized construction estimates;
- uses spreadsheets or files as a major part of the workflow;
- converts estimates into client-facing documents;
- experiences comments, clarifications, or revisions;
- wants Excel compatibility;
- has reusable suppliers, components, rates, templates, or assemblies;
- cares about professional presentation and traceability;
- can identify a real project to test.

### Market category

> **Construction estimating software**

### Product subcategory or explanatory frame

> **An estimate-to-proposal workspace built around reusable cost intelligence.**

### Proposed positioning statement

> **For AEC professionals and teams whose estimates become client-facing proposals and repeated revisions, Archestimator is construction estimating software that keeps the working estimate, Excel output, client review, and cost conversation connected. Unlike copied spreadsheets, detached documents, and generic project tools, it preserves reusable cost knowledge and keeps feedback attached to the number.**

This should be treated as a working sales and strategy statement, not necessarily homepage copy.

## 12. Category Entry Point architecture

This is the largest new opportunity for widening Archestimator’s attack vector while remaining coherent.

| Category Entry Point | Triggering thought or situation | Primary audiences | Message territory | Proof asset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A new project needs an estimate | “We need a credible number.” | Estimators, architects, engineers, contractors | Build a clear estimate without reconstructing the whole workflow | Estimate workspace |
| A bid deadline is approaching | “We need a professional proposal quickly.” | Contractors, estimators, proposal owners | Move from working estimate to client-ready output with less reformatting | Excel export + sample proposal |
| A previous estimate should be reused | “We have done something similar before.” | Estimators, design-build, commercial interiors, suppliers | Start from accumulated cost knowledge rather than a copied file | Library + templates |
| A client needs to understand the costs | “The total alone is not enough.” | Architects, contractors, PMs, clients | Present project costs with clearer structure and context | Shared proposal |
| A client questions the number | “Where did this cost come from?” | Authors, PMs, clients, procurement | Keep questions attached to the proposal being reviewed | Contextual comment |
| Scope or prices changed | “We need to revise this without losing control.” | Estimators, contractors, PMs, CMs | Revise the work while preserving context and history | Revision/activity view |
| Several people need to review | “Everyone is looking at a different file.” | Project teams, proposal owners, procurement | Give participants one current proposal context | Shared review |

### Implication for marketing

The homepage should still lead with one unifying benefit. Campaigns, SEO, role pages, outbound messages, webinars, and product examples can each target a different Category Entry Point.

This is stronger than either extreme:

- listing every possible audience in one hero; or
- redefining the entire company around one persona.

## 13. The switching-forces model

| Force | Archestimator context | Required product or GTM response |
|---|---|---|
| **Push from current method** | duplicated work, manual reformatting, disconnected feedback, overwritten versions, weak traceability | show the complete before/after workflow using a real project |
| **Pull of Archestimator** | connected estimate-to-proposal workflow, client review, reusable knowledge, clearer revisions | demonstrate the magic moment rather than a generic dashboard |
| **Habit of current method** | Excel familiarity, existing formulas, templates, file systems, email, informal practices | preserve Excel output; support import or concierge migration; do not insult the current workflow |
| **Anxiety about switching** | accuracy, loss of control, data safety, client adoption, learning curve, internet reliability, product maturity | deterministic math, visible formulas, sample project, explicit product status, client link with low friction, private-data posture, founder onboarding |

### Adoption doctrine

> **Meet the industry where it works today, then remove the fragmentation around it.**

This is why Excel interoperability is strategic, not merely a feature.

## 14. Activation and First Impact ladder

The corpus currently treats activation, handoff, engagement, and retention as several metrics. V2 should organize them into one causal ladder.

| Level | Event | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Access** | account or invite accepted | The person entered the product; no value proven |
| **Setup** | project and estimate created | The workspace is ready; no meaningful result yet |
| **Operational activation** | first valid total | The estimating engine produced a credible result |
| **Practical activation** | first Excel export or proposal publication | The work became usable outside the editor |
| **First Impact** | a real recipient viewed, commented, asked a question, requested revision, or decided | The product improved a real project interaction |
| **Closed-loop value** | the user revised the estimate or proposal in response | Archestimator proved its connected-workflow differentiation |
| **Retention** | second estimate, later revision cycle, or reuse of cost knowledge | The product is becoming part of recurring work |
| **Expansion** | collaborator invited, multiple projects, shared standards, or broader organization adoption | Value has spread beyond one workflow |

### Recommended headline product metric

> **Weekly active connected cost workflows**

A qualifying workflow should include meaningful estimate creation or revision activity and progress toward a client-facing handoff, response, or decision.

### Recommended differentiated activation metric

> **Percentage of activated workspaces reaching First Impact within fourteen days.**

The exact period remains a hypothesis until real cycle times are observed.

## 15. Growth-loop architecture

### Loop A — Reusable work loop

Estimate completed  
→ components, suppliers, prices, or structures remain reusable  
→ next estimate starts ahead  
→ faster repeated value  
→ more structured knowledge is retained.

### Loop B — Recipient exposure loop

Proposal published  
→ client or stakeholder receives a professional Archestimator experience  
→ recipient interacts with the proposal  
→ recipient becomes aware of the workflow  
→ referral, request, or future adoption becomes possible.

This is a distribution loop. Do not call it a network effect yet.

### Loop C — Team standardization loop

One user builds a successful estimate  
→ invites a collaborator or shares a reusable structure  
→ the team benefits from common templates and context  
→ more projects enter Archestimator  
→ the value of standardization increases.

### Loop D — Proof loop

Real workflow completed  
→ before/after evidence and customer language captured  
→ stronger landing page, content, demo, and sales proof  
→ more qualified users enter  
→ more real workflows are completed.

### Loop E — Private intelligence loop

More structured user-owned estimates and revisions  
→ better project history, price freshness, risk flags, and comparisons  
→ more useful decisions  
→ stronger retention  
→ richer private context.

This is a data-compounding loop inside the customer boundary. It does not require pooling customer data.

## 16. User and buyer architecture

The corpus should stop using “target market” as though everyone has the same relationship to the product.

| Role in adoption | Typical Archestimator participant | Core concern |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily user / author** | estimator, architect, engineer, contractor, proposal owner, supplier | speed, structure, accuracy, reuse, output |
| **Champion** | person most frustrated by the current fragmented workflow | making the change happen and proving value |
| **Workspace owner** | practice owner, preconstruction lead, department lead, contractor owner | standards, visibility, team productivity, client experience |
| **Economic buyer** | firm owner, director, department head, developer or organization leader | business impact, risk, cost, adoption, support |
| **Internal approver** | finance, procurement, IT, management | price, security, compliance, integration, governance |
| **External recipient** | client, developer, owner representative, procurement reviewer | clarity, ease of review, confidence, decision path |
| **Blocker** | entrenched process owner, IT/security, finance, skeptical senior practitioner | switching risk, loss of control, implementation burden |

This map makes it possible to retain a wide market while designing specific journeys, messages, pricing, and proof.

## 17. Revenue architecture and founder-led discovery

### SPICED applied to Archestimator

**Situation**  
How are estimates currently built, exported, sent, reviewed, and revised? Who participates? Which tools and file types are involved?

Where does rework occur? What gets lost? Which handoffs create delay or confusion? What is difficult to explain to clients?

## 18. Pricing and packaging research system

The corpus should preserve the principle that core value should not be arbitrarily punished. It should loosen permanent declarations about a free tier or exact value metric until evidence exists.

### Candidate pricing metrics

| Metric | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Seats** | familiar, administratively simple, aligns with team expansion | may undercapture solo value and discourage collaboration |
| **Workspace or organization** | aligns with shared standards and company value | needs clear size bands |
| **Active projects** | increases with business use | can feel like a tax on the core workflow |
| **Client-sharing and professional output** | directly linked to visible value | gating the handoff may suppress activation |
| **Advanced history, analytics, and governance** | aligns with maturity and organizational value | requires product depth before monetization |
| **Library size** | connected to accumulated value | can feel arbitrary and discourage knowledge capture |
| **Rows, exports, or estimates** | easy to count | should generally be rejected because it punishes productive use |

### Working pricing direction

A strong initial hypothesis is:

- base pricing by user or workspace;
- generous estimating and export behavior;
- paid differentiation around professional branding, client-sharing depth, revision/history intelligence, team standards, analytics, administration, and organization scale.

This is a hypothesis, not canon.

### Required pricing interviews

Ask:

- What outcome would justify paying?
- Who owns the budget?
- What is the cost of the current process?
- Which capability would make the product feel incomplete if removed?
- Which capabilities belong together?
- Which limitation would feel unfair?
- What would be too cheap to trust, a good value, expensive but possible, and too expensive?
- Would the user approve a paid pilot today? If not, what evidence is missing?

## 19. Distinctive brand-asset system

The visual brand should stop being treated as a collection of individual design decisions and become a repeatable asset system.

### Candidate core assets

1. **Archestimator orange** — the primary recognition color.
2. **The mark and wordmark** — always presented consistently.
3. **The connected cost thread** — an orange line or rail linking estimate, proposal, comment, revision, and decision.
4. **The ledger grid** — a technical but refined visual field derived from estimate sheets and cost columns.
5. **The highlighted cost row** — one specific line item carrying source, comment, revision, and decision context.
6. **Editorial numerical typography** — totals, quantities, rates, and issue numbers treated with consistent precision.
7. **A repeated verbal asset** — for example, “Keep the estimate connected” or another founder-approved line.

### Asset rule

> **Use a small number of assets consistently enough that they become recognizable before constantly inventing new visual metaphors.**

### Required future test

Once the brand has market exposure, assess assets for:

- uniqueness;
- recognition;
- correct association with Archestimator;
- use across product, site, proposals, decks, content, and social media.

# Part IV — New Attack Vectors That Stay True to the Product

## 23. Author-led acquisition

Directly recruit people who create estimates and proposals. This is the current primary motion.

## 24. Recipient-led exposure

Treat every shared proposal as a carefully designed product-discovery surface.

Potential mechanisms:

- tasteful “Powered by Archestimator” attribution;
- recipient invitation to learn how the proposal was created;
- optional referral or “use this workflow for your projects” path;
- recipient feedback asking whether the experience was clearer than the usual format;
- organization-level conversation when one client receives proposals from several vendors.

Do not compromise the sender’s professional presentation or turn the proposal into an advertisement.

## 25. Template-led acquisition

Create practical project or workflow templates that demonstrate value and attract specific parts of the market:

- fit-out estimate;
- architectural cost plan;
- specialty-contractor proposal;
- supplier quotation;
- renovation BOQ;
- client revision log.

Templates should feed the same product and brand, not create separate identities.

## 26. Founder-expertise distribution

Use Philip’s architecture and software perspective to build category education:

- what gets lost after an estimate leaves Excel;
- why proposal clarity is an architectural and commercial issue;
- how to structure assumptions and exclusions;
- how to manage revision context;
- why clients need more than a total;
- what software gets wrong about AEC workflows.

The founder should sound like a practitioner building a better method, not a generic SaaS commentator.

## 27. Team-standardization expansion

After individual value is proven, sell team consistency:

- shared cost structures;
- reusable templates;
- common proposal standards;
- visibility into revisions and decisions;
- governance and permissions;
- organization branding.

## 28. Client and developer pull experiments

Keep the owner/developer/procurement platform story out of the main public narrative for now, but run controlled experiments:

- ask recipients whether they would prefer all proposals in this format;
- test whether a developer would request the workflow from vendors;
- test which review records matter to procurement;
- test whether persistent access has standalone value.

This is evidence gathering, not premature repositioning.

## 29. Import and interoperability as acquisition

The migration from spreadsheet to Archestimator is one of the largest adoption barriers. Import, concierge migration, and reliable Excel output can become acquisition mechanisms.

Potential offer:

> **Send us one real estimate. We will rebuild it with you and show how the client review changes.**

That is more credible than a generic product demo.

## 30. Professional and educational channels

Potential later channels include:

- architecture and engineering schools;
- QS and estimating programs;
- professional associations;
- contractor and design-build communities;
- alumni networks;
- supplier and manufacturer education;
- continuing professional development.

Education should create qualified familiarity with the workflow, not a separate low-value education product before the main business works.

# Final Strategic Recommendation

The next version of Archestimator should not become broader in language, more abstract in ambition, or heavier in framework terminology.

It should become more **causal**:

- The market encounters a specific buying situation.
- Archestimator is remembered.
- The customer sees why the current method is no longer enough.
- The product reduces switching anxiety.
- The user reaches a valid estimate.
- The work reaches a real client or stakeholder.
- A response creates First Impact.
- The response becomes a revision without losing context.
- The user returns, reuses prior knowledge, or brings in another participant.
- The company captures evidence, updates its message, and compounds.

The governing strategic line is:

> **Build the brand around the moments that make construction costs difficult. Build the product around the connected workflow that resolves them. Build the company around repeated evidence that the workflow creates impact.**

This widens Archestimator’s attack vector while keeping the product, brand, and startup true to what they already are.
