# Intercom Homepage Grammar — Secondary Study

**Observed:** 2026-07-20 from live official HTML, CSS tokens, and shipped behavior on the [Intercom homepage](https://www.intercom.com/). Intercom is a secondary cue only.

## Current Narrative

1. Fixed navigation.
2. Hero claim, compact explanatory copy, and conversion CTAs.
3. Editorial image relief.
4. Four-state product carousel.
5. Logo wall.
6. Platform/product modules with product and customer proof interleaved.
7. Helpdesk feature groups.
8. Partnership/security/education closure.
9. Testimonial and pricing.
10. Illustrated final CTA and responsive footer.

The useful cadence is `claim → product proof → trust proof`, repeated rather than deferred to the end.

## Observed System

- Hero/final type about `40px` mobile, `64px` tablet, `80px` desktop.
- Major section type about `30/40/54px`; body `16/22px`.
- 12-column grid with `16px` gutters and roughly `1280px` desktop content width.
- Standard section padding about `80px` mobile and `112px` tablet/desktop.
- Product carousel autoplay starts in view and stops offscreen.
- Dense desktop feature carousels become accordions below roughly `896px` rather than miniature dashboards.
- Mobile logo swaps and marquees have explicit reduced-motion behavior.
- Footer link groups become native-feeling disclosures on mobile.

## Useful Secondary Cues

- Provide purpose-built mobile disclosure for complex product proof.
- Use native horizontal snap and disclosures instead of scaling desktop UI down.
- Stop autoplay offscreen and after manual interaction.
- Use FAQ/trust closure to make the early-access commitment understandable.
- Let editorial breathing room interrupt dense product UI.

## Do Not Transfer

No generic AI language, customer imagery, logos, testimonials, pricing/trial mechanics, blue/clay palette, serif brand system, or extreme feature inventory transfers. Attio remains the structural authority.
