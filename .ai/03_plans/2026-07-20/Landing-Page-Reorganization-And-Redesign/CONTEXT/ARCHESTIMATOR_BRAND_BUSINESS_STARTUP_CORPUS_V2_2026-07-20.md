**[WORKING CANON]**

A first valid total proves that the estimating engine works. First Impact proves that Archestimator improved a real construction-cost interaction outside the editor.

## 11. The MVP value loop

**[PRODUCT TRUTH]**

> **Assemble estimate sheets → reuse cost knowledge → export to Excel or publish → share with the client → receive feedback or a decision → revise without disconnecting the conversation → reuse what was learned.**

Reusable components, suppliers, variants, templates, cost history, comments, decisions, and activity records strengthen this loop.

## 12. Why now

**[WORKING CANON]**

> **AEC cost workflows remain heavily spreadsheet- and document-led, while project participants increasingly expect browser-based access, clearer collaboration, and faster decisions. Modern cloud infrastructure can now connect the working estimate to external review without forcing the industry to abandon familiar outputs. The resulting structured, private project context also creates the foundation for future intelligence, while the current product delivers value without depending on AI.**

The “why now” has four parts:

1. **The pain persists:** estimate, proposal, review, and revision remain fragmented across files and messages.
2. **The user expectation has changed:** clients and project teams increasingly expect accessible, current, browser-based information.
3. **The enabling infrastructure exists:** a focused product can now connect internal authoring, external review, realtime activity, files, and structured data without requiring a heavy enterprise deployment.
4. **Structured context has new strategic value:** private, user-owned cost and revision history can support better analysis and future assistance, while deterministic user-controlled estimating remains the foundation.

# Part III — Problem, Insight, and Product Thesis

## 29. The current reality

Construction costs rarely remain inside one spreadsheet. They move through:

- estimate sheets;
- exported Excel workbooks;
- proposal documents;
- emails and messages;
- client questions;
- internal reviews;
- revisions;
- approvals, denials, or requests for change.

At every handoff, context is at risk.

Rates lose their source. Assumptions disappear. Feedback separates from the relevant cost line. Versions are copied or overwritten. The next person receives a document but not the reasoning behind it.

## 30. The problem statement

**[WORKING CANON]**

> **Construction estimates become harder to manage as soon as they leave the working sheet.**

The number travels, but the structure, sources, assumptions, conversation, revision logic, and decision record become fragmented.

## 31. The strongest existing problem line

> **Spreadsheets calculate. They do not remember.**

This remains a strong brand line because it names the limitation without attacking the user or pretending Excel has no value.

## 32. The company insight

**[WORKING CANON]**

> **The estimate is not merely a calculation. It is the working source of a cost proposal, a client conversation, a sequence of revisions, and a project decision.**

Most fragmented workflows treat those as separate artifacts. Archestimator treats them as stages in one connected cost workflow.

## 33. The product thesis

> **Keep the working estimate connected as it becomes an Excel output, a client-facing proposal, a review, a revision, and a decision.**

## 34. The reusable-intelligence thesis

Every completed estimate contains reusable knowledge:

- project structure;
- components and assemblies;
- suppliers and brands;
- rates and price dates;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- sources and notes;
- revision history;
- client questions and decisions.

Archestimator should make that user-owned knowledge easier to preserve and reuse. It must not imply a pooled cross-customer estimating database.

## 35. The connected-workflow thesis

Reusable knowledge creates value before the proposal. Connected review creates value after the proposal.

Together they form the stronger thesis:

> **Each estimate should leave the team with both a clearer current decision and a stronger starting point for future work.**

## 36. Switching forces

Adoption depends on more than the attractiveness of the new product. Archestimator must understand the forces pushing users away from the current method and the forces keeping them attached to it.

| Force | Archestimator context | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| **Push from the current method** | duplicated work, manual reformatting, disconnected feedback, overwritten versions, weak traceability | show the complete before-and-after workflow on a real project |
| **Pull of Archestimator** | connected estimate-to-proposal flow, reusable knowledge, client review, controlled revisions | demonstrate the magic moment rather than a generic dashboard |
| **Habit of the current method** | Excel familiarity, existing formulas, templates, files, email, informal team practices | preserve Excel output, support migration, and respect what already works |
| **Anxiety about switching** | accuracy, loss of control, data safety, client adoption, learning curve, internet reliability, product maturity | deterministic math, visible formulas, explicit product status, sample project, low-friction client link, private-data posture, founder onboarding |

## 37. Adoption doctrine

**[WORKING CANON]**

> **Meet the industry where it works today, then remove the fragmentation around it.**

Excel interoperability is therefore strategic, not merely a feature. The product should preserve familiar outputs while improving authoring, handoff, review, and revision.

## 38. Switching strategy

Early adoption should reduce risk in five ways:

1. **Use a real prior estimate:** prove relevance against the customer’s own work.
2. **Preserve familiar output:** make Excel export dependable and professionally usable.
3. **Create a low-friction recipient experience:** the client should understand the proposal without learning the internal tool.
4. **Make control visible:** show deterministic calculations, access boundaries, activity, and clear product limitations.
5. **Provide concierge migration and onboarding:** remove cold-start effort before expecting self-service behavior.

### User problem

Estimate structures, cost items, and proposal content are repeatedly copied, recreated, or reformatted.

### Benefit

> **Spend less time rebuilding work you have already done.**

### Product proof

- structured estimate hierarchy;
- reusable component library;
- suppliers, brands, variants, and cost history;
- templates and reusable project structures;
- estimate-to-proposal continuity;
- Excel export without rebuilding the document.

## 54. Primary benefit territory: clearer communication

### User problem

Clients and project teams often receive a final document without enough explanation, context, or a clear place to respond.

### Benefit

> **Present project costs in a form people can understand and respond to.**

### Product proof

- client-facing shared proposal;
- selected sheets and structured presentation;
- comments and questions;
- recorded decisions;
- password protection;
- mobile review;
- Excel output for familiar downstream use.

## 55. Primary benefit territory: better control

### User problem

Feedback, changes, and versions separate from the relevant estimate, making it harder to understand what changed and why.

> **Keep the estimate, feedback, and revisions under control as the project changes.**

### Product proof

- activity history;
- row history;
- comments attached to the proposal;
- recipient and publication activity;
- decisions and revision requests;
- restore and historical context;
- planned revision intelligence.

### User problem

Cost decisions slow down when information is unclear or scattered.

### Benefit

> **Move cost decisions forward with clearer information and fewer disconnected handoffs.**

This must not be presented as a guaranteed approval or sales outcome. Archestimator improves the conditions for a decision; it does not guarantee the decision.

## 115. Pricing doctrine

Pricing is part of product strategy. The company should protect activation and productive use while avoiding permanent declarations before evidence exists.

Working principles:

- Prefer understandable subscription pricing.
- Use PHP-denominated pricing for the initial Philippine operating context where appropriate.
- Do not arbitrarily punish the behavior that creates value and retention.
- Treat exact free limits, value metrics, packages, and price points as hypotheses.
- Test who experiences value, who controls budget, and which outcome changes willingness to pay.
- Use paid pilots or manual invoicing before building complex automated billing when legal and tax prerequisites are ready.
- Do not make a low price communicate low quality.
- Do not use false permanent discounts or invented ROI.

## 116. Candidate pricing metrics

| Metric | Strength | Risk or caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Seats** | familiar, simple, aligns with team growth | may undercapture solo value or discourage collaboration |
| **Workspace or organization** | aligns with shared standards and company-level value | requires understandable size bands |
| **Active projects** | scales with business use | can feel like a tax on the core workflow |
| **Client-sharing and professional output** | linked to visible value | gating the handoff can suppress activation and distribution |
| **Advanced history, analytics, and governance** | aligns with maturity and organization value | requires product depth before monetization |
| **Library size** | linked to accumulated value | can discourage the knowledge capture that strengthens retention |
| **Rows, exports, estimates, or revisions** | easy to count | generally weak because it taxes productive core behavior |
