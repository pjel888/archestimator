# DPO and NPC Registration Plan

## DPO Appointment

Create a formal board, owner, or management appointment document naming the DPO or privacy officer.

The appointment must define:

- legal entity;
- appointee;
- authority;
- reporting line;
- independence;
- duties;
- resources;
- confidentiality;
- conflict management;
- escalation authority;
- contact channel;
- effective date;
- replacement procedure.

For a one-person company, the founder may serve initially, but conflicts must be documented and external counsel or an independent reviewer should be available for high-risk decisions.

## DPO Responsibilities

- maintain the ROPA;
- oversee PIAs;
- review lawful bases and notices;
- manage rights requests;
- coordinate breach response;
- maintain registration;
- conduct training;
- review vendors;
- conduct periodic audits;
- report unresolved risks;
- preserve compliance evidence.

## NPC Registration Assessment

Create a signed assessment addressing:

- employee headcount;
- number of data subjects;
- number of sensitive personal information records;
- processing likely to pose risk;
- automated decision-making;
- profiling;
- public access and sharing;
- cross-border processing;
- processor services offered to customers;
- material processing systems;
- current NPC thresholds and categories.

## Required Outputs

- `DPO_APPOINTMENT.md`;
- `NPC_REGISTRATION_ASSESSMENT.md`;
- list of data processing systems;
- registration submission evidence where required;
- annual reassessment;
- change-trigger reassessment;
- public DPO contact details.

## Change Triggers

Reassess registration when:

- user volume materially increases;
- enterprise or government customers launch;
- sensitive-data processing becomes expected;
- payments launch;
- AI profiling launches;
- new processing regions are added;
- substantial new data processing systems launch;
- corporate structure changes.
