## Core Jobs to Be Done

### Job 1 — Build the commercial position

**When** a new project or variation must be priced under time pressure,<br>
**I want to** assemble trustworthy scope, quantities, rates, assumptions, and exclusions from prior knowledge,<br>
**so I can** issue a credible number without rebuilding everything or missing material scope.

- **Functional outcomes:** reduce assembly time; reduce omitted scope; make provenance inspectable; preserve local editability.
- **Emotional outcomes:** feel in control of the number; avoid embarrassment when the client challenges it.
- **Social outcomes:** appear professional and prepared to the client, partner, or approver.
- **Evidence:** `[CODEBASE_FACT | HIGH]` the product implements estimates, rows, reusable components, templates, project sources, and proposal-oriented output. `[RESEARCH_GAP | HIGH]` no first-ICP time-loss or omission baseline exists.

### Job 2 — Secure an attributable decision

**When** a price and scope are ready for external review,<br>
**I want to** send the exact issued commercial position and collect questions and a clear decision against it,<br>
**so I can** proceed without arguing later about which version was seen or accepted.

- **Functional outcomes:** bind discussion to an immutable issue; show decision state; retain attachments and recipients.
- **Emotional outcomes:** reduce anxiety about informal approvals and collections.
- **Social outcomes:** demonstrate disciplined project administration without enterprise software overhead.
- **Evidence:** `[CODEBASE_FACT | MEDIUM]` publication, portal, discussion, and decision capabilities exist in varying states. `[RESEARCH_GAP | HIGH]` recipient adoption and authority comprehension are untested with real external users.

### Job 3 — Revise without erasing history

**When** the client, site, supplier, or design changes after an issue,<br>
**I want to** prepare a new proposal from the accepted state and see a concise deterministic delta,<br>
**so I can** negotiate the change while preserving what was previously agreed.

- **Functional outcomes:** preserve baseline; distinguish pending proposal from accepted state; quantify scope/cost/time effects; resolve or retain assumptions.
- **Evidence:** `[ACTIVE_PLAN | HIGH]` this is the center of the Timeline plan. `[EXTERNAL_PRIMARY_EVIDENCE | HIGH]` AIA, RICS, and established change-order products distinguish proposals, orders/directives, effects, and supporting records. `[PRIMARY_CUSTOMER_EVIDENCE | UNKNOWN]` absent.

### Job 4 — Reconstruct the commercial story

**When** someone disputes, inherits, audits, or tries to collect on a project,<br>
**I want to** reconstruct what was issued, discussed, decided, and superseded,<br>
**so I can** answer quickly with evidence rather than searching files and chats.

- **Functional outcomes:** chronological retrieval; attributable events; exact artifacts; current-state explanation.
- **Risk:** this job can be extremely valuable but infrequent. If users only value it after a dispute, it may not generate the ongoing capture behavior needed for retention.

### Do Timeline and reuse serve the same job?

`[EVIDENCE_BACKED_INFERENCE | MEDIUM]` Timeline and reuse serve **adjacent jobs connected by the estimate**, not one proven indivisible job. Reuse primarily helps Jobs 1 and 7 before issue. Timeline primarily helps Jobs 2–6 and 8 during and after issue. Job 4 is the bridge: a later revision should begin from accepted structured work and preserve why the new state differs.

`[OPEN_DECISION | HIGH]` They become one commercial product only if the same account/buyer adopts both, traversal of the bridge predicts retention, and bundled WTP exceeds the simpler wedge. Otherwise they should be sequenced or separated.

## Switching-Forces Analysis

### Pushes away from the current method

- Recreating estimates and proposal content from old spreadsheets.
- Ambiguous file/version names and forwarded PDFs.
- Approval language dispersed across chat, email, calls, and memory.
- Rework when assumptions or exclusions are not carried into revisions.
- Slow response when clients question a changed price.

These are `[WORKING_HYPOTHESIS | MEDIUM]` until interviews produce recent examples and artifacts.

### Pulls toward Archestimator

- Faster repeat estimating using trusted reusable knowledge.
- One clear issue for the recipient to review.
- A visible accepted baseline and pending change.
- A deterministic explanation of what changed.
- A compact evidence packet for collections, handoff, or dispute prevention.

The first two pulls are likely easier to experience immediately; the latter three require repeated use and disciplined capture.

### Anxieties about switching

- “Will my spreadsheet logic, local rates, and exceptions fit?”
- “Will the client open another portal or just reply in Viber/WhatsApp/email?”
- “Does an in-app approval have real authority?”
- “Will this create more encoding work than it saves?”
- “Can I export and leave with my data?”
- “Is this another all-in-one system I must configure before quoting?”

### Habits holding the current method in place

- Copying a familiar workbook and changing values.
- Treating chat as the fastest coordination channel.
- Printing or emailing a PDF and accepting informal confirmation.
- Recording formal changes only when billing or conflict forces it.
- Relying on one experienced estimator’s memory.

## Strongest Current Connected-Workflow Hypothesis

**Verdict: `HYPOTHESIS_C` — enter through a narrow estimating/proposal wedge, then earn the ledger.**

- **Confidence:** `MEDIUM`.
- **Decisive evidence:** estimating and reusable-source capabilities are live; incumbent products show that estimate-to-change workflows can connect; the full ledger plan has no direct behavioral demand proof and carries substantial adoption burden.
- **Why not Hypothesis A yet:** architecture, category precedent, and professional logic are not proof that one first-ICP buyer will adopt and retain the whole loop.
- **Why not Hypothesis B yet:** the product can generate the record directly from the estimate, reducing a handoff that separate tools create; real pilots are needed to see whether that advantage changes behavior.
- **Missing evidence:** user/buyer identity across stages, event frequency, recipient behavior, second-cycle retention, and paid commitment.
- **Immediate action:** recruit incident-bearing contractors and run artifact-led interviews before expanding the Timeline surface.
- **Stop doing:** treating implementation completeness or plan-pack coherence as demand evidence.
- **Next gate:** the Problem Proof Gate in `09-METRICS-EXPERIMENTS-AND-DECISION-GATES.md`.

## Research Questions That Can Falsify the Thesis

1. Tell me about the last time the amount or scope changed after you sent a quote. What triggered it, and what did you do next?
2. Show the estimate, message thread, revised document, and billing record if available. Which one became the truth?
3. How often did that happen in the last 90 days?
4. What did the incident cost in hours, delay, discount, write-off, or relationship damage?
5. Who prepared the number, who sent it, who approved it, and who paid for the tools?
6. What part of the current workflow would you refuse to move?
7. If a recipient never entered a portal, would an exact issue link plus email/chat notification still solve the problem?
8. Would you maintain assumptions and decisions during a calm project, or only reconstruct them when challenged?
9. What would make you pay this month rather than continue with your spreadsheet and messages?
10. If Archestimator only improved estimating and proposals, would that be sufficient? If it only preserved decisions and revisions, would that be sufficient?

## Decision

`[OPEN_DECISION | HIGH]` Do not promote “living contractual ledger” to a customer-validated category claim. Use it as an internal product-design principle while testing customer language around faster credible estimates, clear revisions, and proof of what changed.
