# Strategy Kernel and No-List

**Research status:** `DESK_AUDIT_COMPLETE / MARKET_VALIDATION_PENDING`

## Diagnosis

### The Core Challenge

- `[CODEBASE_FACT | HIGH]` Archestimator already contains a substantial estimating, reusable-source, publication, and Client Portal product, with additional Timeline domain/schema implementation underway.
- `[RATIFIED_CANON | HIGH]` The Canon correctly acknowledges that implementation is ahead of market-thesis validation.
- `[RESEARCH_GAP | HIGH]` There are no attributable customer interviews, paid design partners, usage cohorts, retention data, or willingness-to-pay commitments in the supplied evidence.
- `[EXTERNAL_PRIMARY_EVIDENCE | HIGH]` Established vendors already offer combinations of estimating, assemblies/templates, portals, change orders, attachments, approvals, revision history, document management, and budget linkage (SRC-C01–SRC-C15).
- `[EVIDENCE_BACKED_INFERENCE | HIGH]` Archestimator’s hypothesized differentiation is therefore not “estimating plus a portal” or “change-order tracking.” It is a simpler, coherent path from reusable estimate knowledge to exact issued proposal, requested change, evidence, decision, and preserved accepted state for an underserved narrow segment.
- `[WORKING_HYPOTHESIS | MEDIUM]` The best initial segment to test is a small Philippine contractor or fit-out firm that creates estimates internally, manages frequent client-driven revisions, lacks enterprise construction software, and has an owner/operator close to both the workflow and budget.

### The Binding Constraint

**Binding constraint:** credible primary evidence that one narrow segment has a frequent, costly, recognized problem and will maintain and pay for the proposed workflow.

This constraint is not solved by:

- more schema completeness;
- a more comprehensive Timeline;
- broader RBAC;
- AI narrative;
- additional document types;
- a richer Assembly object;
- generic market-size estimates; or
- accelerator-framework alignment.

### Strategic Tension

Archestimator has two valuable-looking but unproven compounding loops:

1. **Estimating reuse:** components, templates, prior project sources, linked/copy semantics, price/history knowledge.
2. **Commitment continuity:** issued versions, discussion, decision, accepted baseline, request/evidence/change history.

- `[WORKING_HYPOTHESIS | MEDIUM]` They may form one product because estimate knowledge becomes issued commercial scope and later revisions.
- `[WORKING_HYPOTHESIS | MEDIUM]` They may be separate products because different roles, cadences, buyers, terminology, and incumbent systems own preconstruction and project change control.

The strategy must test this boundary rather than assume it.

## Guiding Policy

### 1. Proceed, but narrow around one observable job

Use the current product as a hypothesis-testing instrument for:

> **Create a credible estimate, issue an exact client-facing proposal, preserve what was accepted, and make the next revision understandable without rebuilding history.**

This is deliberately narrower than the internal “living contractual ledger” thesis.

### 2. Lead with recognized category language

- Entry category: **construction estimating and proposal workflow**.
- Initial value language: **faster revisions, clearer scope, exact issued history, fewer approval misunderstandings**.
- Keep “living contractual ledger,” “Commitment Timeline,” and “Issue” as internal hypotheses until comprehension and buying tests pass.

### 3. Separate current state from chronology

The first surface must answer immediately:

- What is accepted now?
- What is proposed now?
- What changed?
- What evidence and exclusions matter?
- What decision is required?

The chronological Timeline explains how the current state arose; it is not the only way to find current truth.

### 4. Preserve safety architecture; defer breadth

Retain exact issued content, attribution, project/portal ownership, safe artifacts, idempotency, and baseline/proposal separation. These protect users and permit honest testing.

Defer architecture whose only current justification is future breadth:

- many audience types;
- generalized event exposure management;
- complex approval authority;
- broad document-management behavior;
- page-level PDF annotation;
- AI narrative;
- comprehensive workspace command center;
- and complete legacy cutover beyond what is necessary to avoid contradictory user paths.

### 5. Collapse unearned vocabulary

- `[CODEBASE_FACT | HIGH]` “Assembly” is not a live first-class product object; current reusable behavior maps to Components, Templates, and copied Project Sources.
- `[EVIDENCE_BACKED_INFERENCE | HIGH]` Treat historical Assembly behavior as a Template/bundle hypothesis unless primary language tests prove a distinct model.
- Use a small ubiquitous language for the first ICP: **Item/Component, Template, Prior Project, Estimate, Revision, Proposal, Accepted Version, Change Request, Attachment, Decision**.

### 6. Make payment and repeat behavior the expansion gate

Do not interpret prototype success, one completed workflow, or founder-assisted onboarding as validation. Expand only after at least two qualified accounts complete a second natural cycle and make a paid commitment at a pre-stated price.

## Proximate Objective

Within 30–45 days, produce:

1. one evidence-backed first ICP;
2. eight qualified recent-event interviews in that ICP;
3. at least four real sanitized workflow artifacts;
4. six uncoached Timeline/current-state comprehension tests;
5. six realistic capture-burden observations;
6. five recipient behavior tests;
7. the unchanged ₱5,000 Offer V1 presented to five comparable real budget holders;
8. three live pilots; and
9. at least two accounts meeting the acquisition/economic checkpoint with a named, dated second natural cycle.

This 30–45-day checkpoint does not prove retention. The cohort earns the full expansion decision only when at least two accounts become Evidence-Backed Paid Repeat Accounts (`EBPRA`) by the latest precommitted natural-cycle due date. Failure produces an explicit ICP, product-model, or stop decision.

## Coherent Actions

### Action 1 — Select one beachhead hypothesis

Start with small private contractors/fit-out firms rather than a mixed set of architects, QS firms, suppliers, public contractors, and homeowners.

Screen for:

- internally authored estimates;
- at least monthly proposal/revision activity;
- recurring client scope changes;
- spreadsheet/chat/email fragmentation;
- owner/operator access;
- and current spend or material administrative cost.

### Action 2 — Reconstruct the problem before showing the solution

Run recent-event interviews and artifact walkthroughs. Quantify frequency, time, rework, margin, delay, dispute ambiguity, and current spend. Identify proposer, recipient, approver, beneficiary, champion, buyer, and blocker separately.

### Action 3 — Test the smallest valuable release

Use only:

- structured estimate and reusable Templates/Components;
- one exact issued proposal snapshot;
- one current accepted version;
- one pending revision;
- concise deterministic change summary;
- attachments/evidence;
- assumptions/exclusions/unresolved items only when relevant;
- discussion or externally captured response;
- attributable decision receipt.

Do not require every Timeline plan-pack object to test this promise.

### Action 4 — Test recipient reality through normal channels

Send the experience through the recipient’s normal email/chat handoff. Observe whether the stable portal adds clarity or merely adds a login. If recipients stay outside, test an owner-side canonical record with safe PDF/link delivery and attributable external-response capture.

### Action 5 — Resolve Assembly by natural language and behavior

Run card sorts and real estimate-start tasks before designing a new object. Until then:

- call reusable multi-row structures **Templates**;
- call unit-cost records **Components/Items**;
- call copied historical structures **Prior Project Sources**;
- reserve **Rate Build-up** or **Work Package** for segments that use those terms consistently.

### Action 6 — Test a real price

For the first-five gate, offer only the fixed 30-day Offer V1 and its stated post-pilot package in Philippine pesos. After that gate is complete and its result is recorded, use a separately labeled later round to test per-workspace/user against active-project or annual-volume pricing; never mix those alternatives into the first-five sample. External recipients should initially be free because charging them increases adoption friction and competitor references commonly include free/unlimited external portal users.

### Action 7 — Review gates, not opinions

At the end of the cycle, apply the precommitted gates in `09-METRICS-EXPERIMENTS-AND-DECISION-GATES.md`. Preserve negative evidence. Do not explain away missed thresholds with more features.

## Resource Allocation Recommendation

For the next validation cycle:

- **60% — primary research and design-partner operations:** recruiting, interviews, artifact analysis, workflow observation, priced offers.
- **25% — minimum product adaptation:** only defects or friction directly blocking the selected tests.
- **10% — instrumentation and evidence capture:** activation, capture time, channel leakage, repeat cycle, founder intervention.
- **5% — market/competitor maintenance:** update only facts that affect a live deal or positioning decision.

Do not allocate a large parallel team to broad Timeline completion while market evidence remains absent.

## Explicit No-List

Until the relevant gate passes, do **not**:

1. Present “living contractual ledger” as a validated public category.
2. Claim legal enforceability, dispute prevention, entitlement preservation, contract compliance, permanent records, or qualified signatures.
3. Build a generalized contract-management, RFI, submittal, procurement, payment, ERP, or document-management suite.
4. Require all changes to become maximal multi-artifact Issues.
5. Expand page-level PDF comments, graphical redlines, CAD/BIM, OCR, or office-document preview.
6. Add AI comparison or authoritative AI narrative.
7. Add complex sequential, threshold, discipline, or corporate signing-authority workflows.
8. Promote Assembly to a first-class object before the language/reuse gate.
9. Add another reuse concept overlapping Components, Templates, and Project Sources.
10. Claim named estimate versioning or native PDF generation that the current product does not provide.
11. Force recipients to adopt the portal when an owner-side record plus existing channel is more effective.
12. Target architects, contractors, QSs, suppliers, owners, and public agencies simultaneously.
13. Infer Philippine willingness to pay from US vendor prices.
14. Use permits, contractor counts, or economy-wide MSME statistics as a serviceable obtainable market.
15. Scale acquisition, content, or partnerships before repeat paid behavior.
16. Treat free founder-operated pilots as SaaS retention.
17. Finish the entire active Timeline plan solely because it is technically coherent.
18. Rewrite the active implementation plan during this research run.

## Stop Conditions for This Strategy

Change the guiding policy if:

- another segment produces materially stronger recent incidents and paid commitments;
- recipients reject portal participation but owners pay for internal continuity;
- estimate reuse and commitment history consistently split across users/buyers;
- baseline/proposal language remains confusing after two model revisions;
- Assembly naturally resolves to a rate build-up or work package in the selected ICP;
- or the existing estimating-only workflow earns paid repeat use while Timeline does not.

## Red Flags and Data Gaps

- **Red flag:** The active plan pack treats many founder decisions as ratified despite the Canon’s Problem Proof status.
- **Red flag:** Technical completion can create sunk-cost pressure to defend the thesis.
- **Red flag:** The proposed connected workflow may cross preconstruction and contract-administration category boundaries.
- **Data gap:** No beachhead segment has yet passed Problem Proof.
- **Data gap:** No buyer has accepted a price or implementation burden.
- **Data gap:** No recipient behavior or second-cycle retention has been observed.
