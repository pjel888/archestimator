# Primary Research Kit

**Research status:** `DESK_AUDIT_COMPLETE / MARKET_VALIDATION_PENDING`
**Purpose:** Convert the desk audit into attributable customer evidence without leading participants toward the founder thesis.

## Research Objective

- `[RESEARCH_GAP | HIGH]` Archestimator lacks direct evidence about problem frequency, economic severity, workflow ownership, recipient participation, Assembly language, willingness to pay, and repeat use.
- `[EVIDENCE_BACKED_INFERENCE | HIGH]` The next unit of progress is not another broad implementation wave. It is a sequenced set of recent-event interviews, artifact walkthroughs, comprehension tests, live-workflow pilots, and priced commitments.
- `[WORKING_HYPOTHESIS | MEDIUM]` A practical first recruiting funnel is **30 screened prospects → 12 qualified interviews → 6 artifact-backed workflow sessions → 3 active pilots → 2 paid commitments**. This is a planning target, not an industry benchmark or proof threshold by itself.

## Evidence Rules

Count as stronger evidence:

1. A recent, specific incident with date, project, participants, and consequence.
2. A real artifact such as an estimate revision, quotation, change log, email thread, chat export, marked-up plan, approval record, or spreadsheet.
3. A current workaround with actual time, spend, delay, loss, rework, or dispute exposure.
4. An unprompted statement that the problem is actively important.
5. A costly commitment: real project access, sanitized files, staff time, internal introduction, pilot work, payment, or signed purchase intent with budget path.
6. Repeated use on a second natural workflow without founder pressure.

Do not count as validation:

- compliments;
- hypothetical enthusiasm;
- feature requests detached from a recent incident;
- founder explanations followed by agreement;
- willingness-to-pay ranges without a concrete offer;
- role-play or synthetic interviews;
- market-size statistics; or
- technical feasibility.

## Sampling Plan

Use purposive maximum-variation sampling first, not a broad survey.

| Group | Initial target | Why included | Critical contrast |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Solo architect / small practice | 2 | Possible estimator, proposer, project record owner, and buyer in one person | Design-led work versus contractor-led work |
| Contractor / estimator | 3 | High-frequency estimating and change exposure | Small private work versus formal public work |
| Quantity surveyor | 2 | Strong estimate/version discipline and professional vocabulary | Consultant-side versus contractor-side cost control |
| Specialty contractor / fit-out firm | 2 | Repeated scopes, quotation reuse, rapid changes | Repetitive packages versus bespoke work |
| Supplier / fabricator | 1 | Repeated quotations and price volatility | Product quotation versus installed scope |
| Client / developer / owner representative / procurement reviewer | 2 | Recipient behavior and decision comprehension | Professional reviewer versus homeowner/client |
| Economic buyer / practice owner | 2 | Budget, risk, implementation, and buying process | User-beneficiary versus payer |

Recruit across at least two workflow settings:

- private residential / light commercial / fit-out; and
- formal public-infrastructure or professionally administered projects.

Do not mix these into one “construction user” conclusion. Public procurement rules and enterprise controls may produce different jobs, buyers, terminology, and willingness to pay.

## Shared Screener

Ask before describing Archestimator:

1. What is your role, company type, team size, and typical project type/value?
2. In the last 90 days, how many estimates, quotations, variations, or change proposals did you personally create, review, or approve?
3. When was the most recent project where scope, price, quantity, document revision, or requested work changed after an initial estimate or proposal?
4. Which tools were used: spreadsheet, estimating software, accounting system, email, Viber, WhatsApp, Messenger, project-management software, paper, or another tool?
5. Can you discuss one recent event and show sanitized supporting material?
6. Who felt the consequence? Who did the administrative work? Who had approval authority? Who controlled software budget?
7. Are you willing to spend 30 minutes reconstructing the event and, if relevant, 45–60 minutes testing a workflow with sanitized material?

### Universal Inclusion Criteria

- Direct participation in estimating, quotation, scope change, cost review, approval, or project-commercial administration within the last 90 days.
- Ability to describe one recent concrete incident.
- Willingness to discuss current tools and workarounds honestly.
- For artifact sessions: permission to show sanitized or redacted material.

### Universal Exclusion Criteria

- Purely hypothetical interest with no recent relevant workflow.
- Consultants or vendors speaking only from second-hand market knowledge.
- Participants whose only exposure is classroom training.
- Founder friends recruited mainly to be supportive.
- Anyone unable to separate private information safely; do not request confidential material.

## Group-Specific Screeners and Recruiting Messages

### 1. Solo Architect or Small Architecture Practice

**Additional screener**

- Do you prepare cost estimates, fee proposals, construction quotations, or client-facing scope revisions yourself?
- How do you track what a client previously accepted versus what is currently proposed?
- Do you reuse room packages, specifications, estimate rows, templates, or prior projects?

**Include:** principal, project architect, or staff member who personally authors/revises estimates or proposals.<br>
**Exclude:** architects who delegate all commercial documentation and never review it.

**Recruiting message**

> I’m researching how small design practices handle estimate and scope changes after a client has seen an earlier proposal. I’m not selling during the interview. I’d like to reconstruct one recent case, including the tools and documents you actually used. A 30-minute call is enough; sanitized examples are helpful but optional.

### 2. Contractor or Estimator

**Additional screener**

- How many bids or estimates do you create monthly?
- How often does an awarded or active job change after the first quote?
- Do change costs originate from the estimate/budget or get re-entered elsewhere?

**Include:** estimator, owner, PM, or quantity takeoff staff with current responsibility.<br>
**Exclude:** field-only roles with no access to pricing/change records unless recruited specifically as evidence creators.

**Recruiting message**

> I’m studying the actual path from estimate to client-requested change, revised cost, and approval. I’m looking for recent examples—including when the process failed or stayed in chat/email—not opinions about a future product. Could we walk through one case for 30 minutes?

### 3. Quantity Surveyor

**Additional screener**

- Are you consultant-side, contractor-side, or owner-side?
- Which cost-plan, BOQ, variation, and valuation stages do you handle?
- How are revision basis, time impact, source documents, and authority recorded?

**Include:** QS with recent pre- or post-contract change work.<br>
**Exclude:** QS roles limited to unrelated valuation work with no estimate/change history.

**Recruiting message**

> I’m researching where estimate and variation records lose context across revisions, documents, and approvals. I’d value a critical professional walkthrough of one recent change—not a product pitch—and especially the terms your team actually uses.

### 4. Specialty Contractor or Fit-Out Firm

**Additional screener**

- Which scopes repeat enough to reuse components, build-ups, rooms, or packages?
- How are material/labor prices refreshed?
- How often do site conditions or design revisions change your quote?

**Include:** trade estimator, owner, PM, or commercial lead with repeated quotation work.<br>
**Exclude:** firms with no repeatable scope and no recent changes unless used as a contrast case.

**Recruiting message**

> I’m studying how specialty and fit-out teams reuse prior estimating knowledge and then document revisions or client changes. I’d like to see what you really copy, rebuild, or avoid using in one recent quote. The session is research, not a sales demo.

### 5. Supplier or Fabricator

**Additional screener**

- Do you produce repeated configured quotations or bills of materials?
- What changes most often: quantity, specification, finish, delivery, installation, or price?
- How do you preserve what was included in each issued quotation?

**Include:** quotation owner, estimator, sales engineer, or fabricator with configurable scope.<br>
**Exclude:** simple catalogue sellers with no meaningful quotation assembly or revision process.

**Recruiting message**

> I’m researching how repeated quotations are assembled, revised, and approved when specifications or quantities change. I’d like to reconstruct one recent quote and understand the real tools and terminology. No preparation beyond recalling the case is required.

### 6. Client, Developer, Owner Representative, or Procurement Reviewer

**Additional screener**

- Have you reviewed an estimate, variation, change order, or revised proposal in the last 90 days?
- What did you need before deciding?
- Which channel did you actually use to ask questions or approve?

**Include:** real recipient/reviewer with recent decision experience.<br>
**Exclude:** participants who only received a final invoice and never reviewed scope/cost changes.

**Recruiting message**

> I’m researching how clients and project reviewers understand revised scope, supporting documents, exclusions, and requested decisions. I’d like to reconstruct one recent review and later test whether a proposed record is understandable without explanation. This is not a legal assessment or sales call.

### 7. Economic Buyer or Practice Owner

**Additional screener**

- Who approves software and process changes?
- What budget or existing spend could a product replace?
- What implementation, security, or client-adoption risk can block purchase?

**Include:** owner, managing partner, commercial director, operations lead, or delegated budget holder.<br>
**Exclude:** users with no influence over budget unless paired with the actual buyer.

**Recruiting message**

> I’m researching whether reducing estimate rework and improving change records is important enough to fund. I’m not asking for a general opinion. I’d like to understand a recent costly incident, current spend, the buying path, and what a concrete pilot would have to prove.

## 30-Minute Mom Test Interview Guide

### 0–3 minutes: Context

1. What is your role in the project and commercial workflow?
2. What kinds of projects do you handle most often?
3. What did you personally do last week related to estimating, quoting, revisions, or approvals?

### 3–15 minutes: Recent-Event Reconstruction

Ask for the last relevant event, not the worst or ideal event.

1. When did the event start, and what triggered it?
2. What was the current accepted or expected scope immediately before it?
3. Who first requested, noticed, or instructed the change?
4. What exact words or document were used?
5. What did you do next, step by step?
6. Which files, chats, emails, spreadsheets, calls, or systems were touched?
7. Where did numbers get copied or re-entered?
8. How did you know which estimate/document revision was current?
9. What remained uncertain about cost, time, scope, authority, or responsibility?
10. How was the decision recorded? What happened afterward?
11. What did someone later have to reconstruct?

### 15–21 minutes: Cost, Frequency, and Consequence

1. How many times has something similar happened in the last month/quarter/year?
2. How many people and hours did this instance consume?
3. Did it delay approval, work, payment, procurement, or handover?
4. Was there rework, margin loss, write-off, underbilling, overpayment, claim exposure, or client friction?
5. What is the most defensible numeric estimate of the consequence?
6. Who cared enough to intervene, and what did they do?
7. What was merely annoying versus materially costly?

### 21–25 minutes: Current Alternative and Switching Forces

1. What do you use today, including manual tools and people?
2. What have you tried before? Why did you stop?
3. What works well enough that you would not want to lose it?
4. What would make a switch risky?
5. Who else would have to change behavior?
6. If nothing changed for a year, what would happen?

### 25–28 minutes: Buyer and Budget

1. Who owns the problem operationally?
2. Who benefits immediately?
3. Who approves a purchase?
4. Which budget would pay: estimating, PM, operations, finance, IT, project overhead, or owner cost?
5. What do you already spend on software, admin labor, outsourced estimating, document control, or rework?
6. What proof would the buyer require?

### 28–30 minutes: Commitment Ask

Choose the next costly commitment appropriate to evidence maturity:

- show a sanitized artifact;
- introduce the actual recipient or buyer;
- schedule a 60-minute workflow observation;
- permit testing on one real upcoming revision;
- join a 2–4 week pilot;
- or review a priced pilot offer and make a real decision.

Do not end with “Would you use this?”

## Evidence-Request Checklist

Request only sanitized material the participant is authorized to share:

- original estimate/quotation and one later revision;
- marked-up plan or specification revision;
- change request, instruction, proposal request, or variation record;
- email/chat thread showing handoffs;
- approval/rejection/changes-requested evidence;
- change register or log;
- spreadsheet/version naming pattern;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- cost/time impact analysis;
- artifact package sent to the recipient;
- invoice/payment consequence;
- time log or reconstruction estimate;
- current software invoice or budget range;
- template/component/library example;
- prior-project material reused in a new estimate.

Record what was offered but unavailable. Absence itself is not proof of a product need; it may reflect privacy, low importance, or weak process.

## Concept and Usability Tests

### Timeline Comprehension Test

**Stimulus:** A realistic project with an accepted baseline, one pending issue, a client request, evidence, revised estimate, one replaced artifact, assumptions, exclusions, unresolved time impact, discussion, and decision state. Do not explain product terms first.

**Tasks**

1. “What is currently accepted?”
2. “What is only proposed?”
3. “What changed, and compared with what?”
4. “Why did it change?”
5. “Which evidence supports it?”
6. “Which assumptions apply to this proposal?”
7. “What is excluded?”
8. “What remains unresolved?”
9. “Who is being asked to do what?”
10. “If the recipient accepts, what becomes current?”
11. “Where would you go to understand an older decision?”

**Pass signal:** Correct answers without founder explanation, using the participant’s own words, with no material confusion between request, proposal, directive, accepted baseline, or current working estimate.

### Capture-Burden Test

**Stimulus:** Real sanitized project material, not a polished toy example.

Observe the proposer creating a complete official record. Capture:

|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Create request/record | | | | | | | | |
| Attach evidence | | | | | | | | |
| Select estimate/artifact versions | | | | | | | | |
| State cost/scope/time effect | | | | | | | | |
| State assumptions/exclusions | | | | | | | | |
| Record unresolved items | | | | | | | | |
| Configure recipient/decision | | | | | | | | |
| Publish and follow up | | | | | | | | |

**Failure signal:** The user must reconstruct information they do not normally possess, performs net-new admin without offsetting another task, or returns to chat/email because publication is too heavy.

### Recipient Adoption Test

Give a recipient a real issue through the intended channel. Do not coach.

Observe whether they:

- open it;
- understand state;
- ask questions inside or outside the portal;
- request a PDF/email summary;
- forward the link safely;
- make a decision;
- trust the provenance;
- and return later without prompting.

Record channel leakage as primary evidence, not user error.

### Assembly Terminology Test

Use cards/examples representing:

- Component;
- Assembly;
- Template;
- prior Project Source;
- Group;
- Area;
- work package;
- rate build-up;
- room package; and
- system/package of work.

Tasks:

1. Sort items that belong together.
2. Name each group in the participant’s natural language.
3. Predict what happens when inserted into an estimate.
4. Predict what happens when a source price changes.
5. Say which objects should be reusable, versioned, linked, copied, or project-specific.

Do not reveal “Assembly” until after natural naming is captured.

**Pass signal:** At least six of eight target estimating users independently recognize a distinct reusable concept, and at least five choose it over Templates or Project Sources for a real task. The canonical gate is in `09-METRICS-EXPERIMENTS-AND-DECISION-GATES.md`.

### Reuse Behavior Test

Ask the participant to start a realistic estimate. Offer the current alternatives neutrally: individual components, template, prior project, lightweight bundle, rate build-up, scope package, or blank estimate.

Observe:

- first choice;
- search strategy;
- trust checks;
- price-freshness checks;
- editing behavior;
- provenance needs;
- and whether the reused object remains conceptually coherent after insertion.

### Connected-Loop Test

One participant or real team must:

1. start from reusable estimating knowledge;
2. create a project estimate;
3. publish a client-facing proposal/issue;
4. receive a real change request;
5. revise cost and evidence;
6. obtain a decision; and
7. start another estimate or revision cycle.

Ask after each stage: “What product do you think you are using now?” Confusion or separate ownership may indicate two products, not one connected workflow.

### Economic Test

Present a concrete offer, not a feature wishlist.

### Precommitted Offer V1 — Paid Problem-Proof Pilot

Present this unchanged to the first five comparable qualified economic buyers:

- **Pilot price:** `₱5,000` one-time, paid before setup, for 30 days.
- **Post-pilot price:** `₱2,500/month` per company workspace, monthly cancellation.
- **Included:** one company workspace; up to three internal users; unlimited external recipients; one live project; one Template or prior-project source import; one estimate → issue → recipient decision → revision cycle; standard XLSX/browser-print outputs; up to three founder hours for setup, training, and ordinary support.
- **Excluded:** custom integrations, bespoke feature development, historical data cleanup beyond the one source import, legal/contract advice, guaranteed dispute prevention, and founder operation of the customer’s workflow.
- **Success measures:** complete the real loop, median ordinary net-new capture at or below ten minutes, unaided recipient action, and one natural second cycle where cadence permits.
- **Data boundary:** only customer-authorized/sanitized project material; standard product access/security controls; no weakening of scan, tenant, or record-integrity rules.
- **Cancellation/refund:** no renewal obligation; refund only if a verified Archestimator defect prevents the agreed live workflow and no reasonable workaround or extension is accepted. A refunded pilot does not pass the Economic Gate.

`[WORKING_HYPOTHESIS | LOW]` These amounts are experimental offer terms, not validated pricing or a public recommendation. Fixing them before the first five offers prevents post-hoc discounting and makes rejection evidence interpretable.

Ask the real budget holder for one decision: pay, sign a time-bounded purchase commitment contingent on stated proof, decline, or identify the exact approval step and date. “Interested” is not a pass.

## Willingness-to-Pay Protocol

1. Complete problem and current-spend discovery before discussing price.
2. Identify economic buyer, budget owner, approval path, procurement/security blockers, and competing spend.
3. Present Offer V1 unchanged to the first five comparable participants; do not vary it opportunistically after each objection.
4. After the five-offer gate is complete, test alternative per-project or annual-volume packaging in a separately labeled round; do not mix rounds.
5. Ask for the `₱5,000` paid pilot or a dated purchase commitment to the same terms, not a 1–10 rating.
6. Before revealing the fixed price, ask: “At what monthly price would this feel acceptable/good value?”, “At what price would it feel expensive but still worth considering?”, “At what price would it be prohibitive?”, and “At what price would it feel too cheap to trust?” Record numbers and reasoning; do not use them as payment evidence.
7. After revealing the price, ask what exact budget pays and what current software, labor, rework, or risk response it replaces.
8. Record objection category: no problem, wrong user, no budget, no urgency, insufficient proof, implementation burden, recipient resistance, security/legal, price metric, or price level.
9. Do not discount before the five-offer round ends. Record a declined offer as a decline.
10. Treat the included three founder hours and any extra founder service separately from software willingness to pay.

## Transcript Rubric

Score each interview 0–2 on each dimension.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recency | No concrete event | Event older than 6 months or vague | Specific event within 90 days |
| Frequency | One-off/unknown | Occasional | Repeated at natural workflow cadence |
| Economic consequence | None/guess | Time or risk described, weak number | Defensible cost, delay, loss, or spend |
| Active workaround | None | Informal workaround | Repeated process/tool/spend already in use |
| Recognition | Led agreement | Recognized after prompt | Unprompted active concern |
| Artifact proof | None | Partial/second-hand | Sanitized primary artifact shown |
| Buyer clarity | Unknown | Influencer known | Budget holder and path identified |
| Switching pressure | No trigger | Conditional interest | Active search, recent switch, or deadline |
| Commitment | Compliment | Follow-up meeting | Artifact, introduction, pilot, or payment |
| Disconfirming evidence | Avoided | Mentioned | Specific reasons the thesis may fail |

Interpretation:

- **16–20:** strong interview-level evidence; still not segment validation alone.
- **11–15:** meaningful but incomplete; pursue artifacts and workflow observation.
- **6–10:** weak problem evidence or wrong participant.
- **0–5:** exclude from positive conclusions.

Never average away contradictions. Tag each claim and preserve negative cases.

## Research Safety and Integrity

- Obtain consent before recording.
- Minimize personal and project data; accept redacted artifacts.
- Do not request material the participant is not authorized to share.
- Store identity separately from analytical notes where practical.
- Never imply legal advice, contract validity, or confidentiality beyond the actual research agreement.
- Preserve exact negative evidence and participant language.
- Do not teach product vocabulary before terminology/comprehension tasks.
- Separate founder-assisted success from self-serve product behavior.

## Red Flags and Data Gaps

- **Red flag:** Recruiting only founder-adjacent architects would confirm founder language and miss contractor/QS/buyer/recipient differences.
- **Red flag:** A polished prototype can create admiration without demonstrating maintenance behavior.
- **Red flag:** Free pilots may prove interest in founder service rather than software value.
- **Data gap:** No actual participant pool, consent process, or interview schedule is currently recorded.
- **Data gap:** No real priced offer has been tested with a budget holder.
- **Data gap:** No second-cycle retention has been observed.
