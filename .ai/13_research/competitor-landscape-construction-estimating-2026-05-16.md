# Competitor Landscape — Construction Estimating Tools

**Date accessed:** 2026-05-16
**Purpose:** Identify competitors and positioning gaps for Archestimator.

## Archestimator target context

Archestimator aims to help contractors create construction cost estimates quickly and easily while managing multiple projects and design changes.

## Main competitor categories

### 1. Digital takeoff + estimating platforms

Representative products:

- STACK — https://www.stackct.com/pricing/
- PlanSwift — https://www.planswift.com/pricing/
- Bluebeam Revu — https://www.bluebeam.com/pricing/
- Autodesk Takeoff — https://www.autodesk.com/products/autodesk-takeoff/overview
- eTakeoff / RSMeans integrations — https://www.rsmeans.com/products/online

Positioning:

- Measure from plans/PDFs
- Generate material/labor quantities
- Create bids/proposals

Strengths:

- Strong PDF/plan takeoff workflows
- Area/length/count measurement
- Plan markup and collaboration
- Useful for estimators and subcontractors bidding from drawings

Weaknesses/gaps:

- Can be setup-heavy
- Often takeoff-first, not revision-first
- May still require manual cost databases and proposal formatting
- Pricing can be high for small contractors
- Bluebeam is powerful for markup but not a full estimating engine by itself

Pricing found:

- PlanSwift Professional: US$2,000, including one year of updates/support
- Bluebeam Basics: US$260/user/year
- Bluebeam Core: US$330/user/year
- Bluebeam Complete: US$440/user/year
- STACK: public page requires building/seeing pricing; no clear numeric public price found in quick pass
- Autodesk Takeoff: source page was blocked during research; verify separately

Implication for Archestimator:

Do not compete head-on as another heavy takeoff tool. Compete on faster estimate creation, revision handling, contractor-friendly workflows, and transparent assumptions.

### 2. Contractor estimating + job management platforms

Representative products:

- Buildxact — https://www.buildxact.com/us/pricing/
- Contractor Foreman — https://contractorforeman.com/plans/
- Procore — https://www.procore.com/pricing
- Buildertrend
- Houzz Pro
- JobTread
- CoConstruct / BuildBook adjacent category

Positioning:

- End-to-end contractor business management
- Lead → estimate → proposal → job management → change order → invoice

Strengths:

- Broader workflow coverage
- Good for contractors who want one business platform
- Estimates, POs, RFQs, change orders, client portals, scheduling, accounting integrations

Weaknesses/gaps:

- Can be overbuilt for fast estimating
- Onboarding/setup burden
- Enterprise platforms like Procore are likely too heavy for small contractors
- Pricing and workflows may not fit local/regional contractors

Pricing found:

- Buildxact Foundation: US$199/month
- Buildxact Pro: US$399/month
- Buildxact Master: US$599/month
- Buildxact Blu Estimate Generator add-on: +US$99/month
- Buildxact Blu Takeoff Assistant add-on: +US$99/month
- Buildxact Blu Estimate Reviewer add-on: +US$149/month
- Contractor Foreman: page positioning says starts at US$49/month
- Procore: custom quote

Implication for Archestimator:

Avoid becoming a full contractor-management suite too early. Make the estimate/change-order wedge excellent first.

### 3. Quote/invoice apps for small contractors and trades

Representative products:

- Joist — https://www.joist.com/pricing/
- Jobber
- Invoice Simple
- QuickBooks Estimates
- Tradify / ServiceM8 adjacent trade apps

Positioning:

- Fast estimates/invoices
- Signatures, deposits, payments, client profiles

Strengths:

- Very easy onboarding
- Affordable
- Good for simple line-item estimates
- Mobile-friendly

Weaknesses/gaps:

- Weak/no takeoff
- Weak BOQ generation
- Weak cost database and assembly logic
- Not ideal for complex building projects or design revisions

Pricing found:

- Joist Basics: US$10/month
- Joist Pro: US$16/month
- Joist Elite: US$32/month

Implication for Archestimator:

Archestimator must feel almost as easy as these tools, but more powerful for building estimates, BOQs, and revisions.

### 4. Cost database / BOQ / QS tools

Representative products:

- RSMeans Data Online / Gordian — https://www.rsmeans.com/products/online
- Craftsman Book / National Estimator
- RIB CostX — https://www.rib-software.com/en/products/rib-costx
- Trimble WinEst
- Sage Estimating
- Candy / CCS
- Cubit / Buildsoft

Positioning:

- Detailed estimating and cost planning
- Cost databases, assemblies, QS workflows, benchmarking

Strengths:

- Strong cost data and formal estimating workflows
- Useful for detailed cost plans and benchmark estimates
- RSMeans has material/labor/equipment breakdowns, localized cost factors, and quarterly updates for North America

Weaknesses/gaps:

- Often expensive or quote-based
- Can be too formal/slow for small contractors
- Cost data may not fit the Philippines or local contractor reality
- Requires training and discipline

Implication for Archestimator:

Transparent local cost assumptions and editable contractor price libraries could be a major differentiator, especially outside North America.

### 5. Remodeling / residential builder estimating tools

Representative products:

- Clear Estimates — https://www.clearestimates.com/pricing
- Buildxact
- Buildertrend
- Houzz Pro
- JobTread

Positioning:

- Fast estimates and proposals for remodelers/builders
- Prebuilt cost data/templates

Strengths:

- Contractor-friendly
- Proposal-oriented
- Faster setup than enterprise tools

Weaknesses/gaps:

- Often US-centric
- Not necessarily designed for Philippine local pricing
- May not handle frequent design revisions elegantly

Pricing found:

- Clear Estimates Standard: US$79/month
- Clear Estimates Pro: US$119/month
- Clear Estimates higher/custom-branded tier: about US$249/month from public page extraction

Implication for Archestimator:

A localized, revision-first, easy estimate/proposal workflow can compete if it is simpler and more regionally adaptable.

## Philippine / regional competitor note

Quick research did not reveal a clear Philippines-native SaaS leader for contractor estimating.

Likely current alternatives in the local market:

- Excel BOQ templates
- manual QS workflows
- supplier quotations
- PDF/CAD takeoff tools
- global tools used through local resellers/training
- informal cost databases from prior projects

This is uncertain and requires deeper validation through:

- Philippine contractor interviews
- construction Facebook groups
- local QS/contractor software resellers
- material supplier price-list behavior
- Lazada/Shopee/retail pricing reality checks
- architects/contractors who already use PlanSwift, Bluebeam, Cubit, CostX, or Excel

## Strategic whitespace

The strongest opening is:

> Fast contractor-first estimates and revision updates without the overhead of traditional takeoff software or enterprise construction-management platforms.

Archestimator should differentiate on:

- speed
- ease of use
- design-change responsiveness
- editable local cost libraries
- contractor-ready BOQ/proposal exports
- transparent assumptions
- confidence/uncertainty flags
- reuse of previous project templates and line items

## Competitive risks

- Established tools are adding AI features.
- Bluebeam/PlanSwift/Excel habits are entrenched.
- Contractor-management platforms may bundle estimating deeply.
- Invoice apps may move upward into quoting/estimating.
- AI estimates will fail if users do not trust quantities, prices, or assumptions.

## Positioning draft

> Archestimator is a fast AI-assisted construction estimating workspace for contractors dealing with changing designs. It helps turn scope notes, templates, and project revisions into editable BOQs, quote deltas, and client-ready proposals without the overhead of traditional takeoff software.
